Solar Pasteurizer (WAPI)

A simple, low-cost device that uses a small wax indicator to confirm when water has been safely pasteurized using solar heat. No fuel, no electricity, and no moving parts required.

Difficulty ⬤◯◯ Basic
Build time 1–2 days
Estimated cost USD 2–5
Treats ~1 L per cycle
Tools required Scissors, needle
Developed by GHE student project

How it works

The WAPI (Water Pasteurization Indicator) is a small plastic tube containing a food-grade wax with a melting point of 70°C. The tube is suspended in the water container during solar heating. When the wax melts and floats to the top, the water has reached pasteurization temperature and is safe to drink — even if it has not boiled.

Solar pasteurization requires sustained temperatures above 65°C for several minutes, which kills all major waterborne pathogens including bacteria, viruses, and protozoa. The WAPI provides a reliable visual indicator without any electronic components.

Materials

  • Small plastic tube (3–5 cm)
  • Food-grade wax (beeswax or soy)
  • Nylon fishing line (30 cm)
  • Small weight (nut or washer)
  • Cork or stopper

Tools

  • Scissors or craft knife
  • Small saucepan (for wax melting)
  • Needle or thin wire
  • Heat source (stove or fire)

Key steps

  • Melt wax and pour into tube
  • Insert fishing line while wax is liquid
  • Allow to cool and set
  • Attach weight to lower end of line
  • Seal top with cork
  • Calibrate with boiling water test
